# UI rules v0.8

Purpose: an agent building any of the owner's interfaces produces a clean surface whose components stay out of the way until needed, with cool accents used sparingly.

Words follow `core/rules/TERMS.md`. Values come from section 15 of `templates/html/ui-handbook.html`, and a value outside that table is not used. Every rule has a working sample in the handbook.

1. **Choose a theme pair first.** Pure/Neon, Prism/Aurora or Paper/Ember. The light/dark switch moves within the pair. Never pair black with cobalt. A theme is one entry in `core/tokens/themes.json`; components read tokens and never name a theme.
2. **Keep the base neutral.** Only Signal, Volume and Pattern carry colour or material: one Signal per region, at most one Volume per screen, colour on Pattern only for data. In Neon, `#84cc16` is the only interface colour; images are content and keep their own colour. Category colours appear in the coloured themes only. Story Forge Create alone has the semantic track-colour exception in decision [0010](../../docs/decisions/0010-story-forge-timeline-monitor-and-chat.md), decision 18: voice, sound, music, subtitles and graphics use distinct `trk-*` colours in every theme, including Pure and Neon, only for their lane labels, dots and clips. This is not permission for other interface colours or other samples.
3. **Use the radius tiers by role.** Control 9, card 16, panel 22, capsule 999, joined 0.
4. **Use few lines, and give each line a job.** Regions separate by background and depth. A line is drawn only for one of these jobs, each shown in the handbook: keyboard focus; active or invalid input; a drop target; and a divider between same-coloured regions. A line without one of these jobs is a defect, and so is a job that draws two lines. Selected and running components are shown by background and elevation, never by a line. Examples: a selected node changes to the Selected background with a higher shadow and has no ring; a focused input shows only its own inner 1.5px line, with no outline outside it; the current theme card is shown by its width, so it has no outline. See decision 0012.
5. **Depth states a level.** Flat, lifted (hover), float, recess, raised, volume. Depth never decorates. Shadows come only from the elevation tokens `--e1` to `--e4` and the hover token `--lift`: at most two layers, blur at most 48px. A shadow is never cut by its container: give the clipping parent room or do not clip.
   A selected or hovered surface is always opaque. Translucency belongs only to glass overlays.
6. **Give each component a core and four states, through the UX layer.** Rest shows only the core. Hover responds after .15s: the component gains a shadow and its tools, and if it is interactive, every other component on the surface dims to .3, unless rule 27's switch is off. When the pointer leaves, the others recover. A click on the component is not needed for any of this. A component is interactive if it holds a control or changes in response to the pointer or a click; `core/behavior/ux.js` detects controls, and a component with no control that still changes declares `data-live` (a node, a theme card, a hover demo). A display-only component, such as an explanation card or a KPI card, only gains a shadow: others do not dim and it takes no focus. Every sample declares this with `data-scope`, `data-cmp` and `data-tool` and runs on `core/behavior/ux.js`; no sample writes its own version. A surface opts out only with `data-dim="none"`. The top bar does not dim the page, so a theme can be judged while it is being chosen. Story Forge Create alone follows decision [0010](../../docs/decisions/0010-story-forge-timeline-monitor-and-chat.md), decisions 14 and 20: the browser and monitor never dim from timeline or chat attention. Watching the monitor is a deliberate mode change: hovering the timeline only shows its tools, and a click on it holds focus until a click outside or Esc, which enlarges the monitor and temporarily pushes the side panel away (decision 0014). Chat activation changes only the chat. The UX layer still owns attention; this exception does not change other components or samples. Grey (`--sel`) always means selected. `--mark` means being read, in the Story Forge script while the playhead moves, following decision [0015](../../docs/decisions/0015-script-selection-and-reading.md).
7. **Only an expanded component keeps others dim.** A component whose click expands content or takes space (`data-hold`) keeps others dim after the pointer leaves, until a click outside or Esc, because the expansion still occupies the surface. Example: the chat with its history open. A component that expands nothing behaves the same whether clicked or not: others dim while the pointer is on it and recover when it leaves. Example: a calendar card. The focus scope lists everything belonging to the component (input, send, history, menus).
8. **Use the attention lock only between components that claim the whole surface.** Declared with `data-lock`: the next is admitted 500ms after the previous leaves. Example: the workspace timeline and chat. On a board of adjacent cards the lock would add half a second to every move, so each card responds after the .15s intent delay alone.
9. **Choose how to give way by the relation.** Push when both are read together, Overlay when the view is temporary (add scroll room so content can clear it), Retire when both claim the same place. A change never disturbs a component it has no relation to. Example of Push: clicking Income on the dashboard widens it to 68% and moves the other cards into a side column, while the board keeps its height and the cards stay usable. A data table row's detail pushes the rows below it the same way.
10. **Animate with the table.** Main easing `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`. A hovered component does not move: it only gains a shadow, in .2s through `--lift`. Module in and out .35s, tools .25s, input expansion .5s, push .52s. Respect reduced motion.
11. **Lay out by what is read first.** Pick one of hero, bands, main with aside, full-bleed with floats, kanban, swimlanes. Large numbers stand apart; small labels sit tight.
12. **Give touch an equal path.** Hover does not exist on touch: tools stay visible and focus comes from a tap.
13. **Hide fully or not at all.** A hidden tool also leaves hit-testing (`visibility:hidden`), and it becomes hittable only when it becomes visible, so the pointer never changes over nothing. A component's clickable and focus bounds equal its visible bodies, including any visible menu or history; blank space beside them is outside, even when a wrapper spans it.
14. **Draw links as a system.** A link starts at the edge of the thing that owns it and ends at a visible end point. Weight 1.3 to 2px, round caps, a gradient from source to target. Business flows use rounded elbows with arrowheads, and cross-group flows are dashed. Only the links of the current selection are coloured and carry the moving pulse.
15. **Colour relates.** A core, its link and its detail share the current category colour. Coloured themes use muted category colours of equal weight. Only Story Forge Create's semantic timeline track colours follow the all-theme exception in rule 2 and decision [0010](../../docs/decisions/0010-story-forge-timeline-monitor-and-chat.md), decision 18: a lane label, its dot and its clips share one colour; each of the five track colours reaches 4.5:1 on black, and `trk-on` reaches 4.5:1 on each. Other categories and samples keep this rule unchanged.
16. **Lay out with density and stagger.** Body 13, card padding 12/14, card gap 10, section gap 44. Spans differ on a 12-column bento so priority is visible; never a row of equal boxes when one matters more.
17. **Set each language on its own terms.** Chinese glyphs fill their box, so the same size reads denser: Chinese takes every vertical padding and margin at 1.3 times the English value through one token, `--y`; line height 1.65, tracking +.012em, headings at weight 600, labels at 11px in natural case. English: `--y` 1, line height 1.45, headings at weight 680 with -.022em tracking, labels uppercase and tracked. Labels, tags and buttons have a fixed line height so they are the same height in both. Switching language cross-fades the page and pins the first visible element at its screen position, so the layout change is never seen as a jump.
18. **Every screen has a phone form.** Check at 390 wide. The arrangement may change; the language may not.
19. **A selector slides one indicator.** Every group of options (segmented control, recess switch, language, device, theme dots, calendar day, timeline view) has one indicator that slides between options and follows them if they resize. It moves by CSS transition on `transform`, in .3s with the main easing, so a busy main thread cannot stall it; `core/behavior/ux.js` only sets its target. Compact options keep their positions when selected, so the next click lands where the pointer already is. Example: the six theme dots keep their places and show their names as a tip. Theme cards are previews rather than compact options, so the current one is wider; it carries no outline.
20. **Nothing is lost to an animation.** A click during a page transition ends the transition and reaches the element under the pointer. A control keeps its position under `:active`: centring uses `translate`, so a press `transform` cannot move it away from the pointer. A press-and-hold captures the pointer, shows its progress, and retracts on early release.
21. **Page chrome follows the same rules.** The navigation rail rests as faint ticks in its own gutter, clear of the content. On hover it expands over the page as frosted glass and the page dims to .3; when the pointer leaves, the page recovers at once. Anything floating over content (menus, command list, phone navigation) is frosted glass. The top bar keeps its surface opaque enough that content under it never competes with its labels.
22. **Never cut content with a hard edge.** Content clipped by a container fades out over about 20px through `mask-image`, only when measurement shows that it actually overflows. Remove the mask when it fits; complete text and chips never fade merely because a container can scroll. Example: the collapsed ledger, the agent log, the phone panel.
23. **A dragged item takes its place while it moves.** The item floats under the pointer and a recessed slot stays in the list. The slot moves to the nearest position as soon as the item reaches it, the others slide out of the way, and counts update, so the result is visible before release. Release only drops it into the slot, and Esc returns it to its origin. Example: dragging a kanban card onto another column. Every sortable list uses `UX.sortable`; no sample uses the browser's own drag or writes its own version.
24. **A visible state ends with what started it.** Keep a tool or value open only while the pointer is there or for `:focus-visible`, never `:focus-within`, because a mouse click on a button would hold it open. A press never moves a target before release: a control beside something that closes on press carries `data-keep`. One state has one owner, and clearing a selection clears everything it lit (outline, pill, panel). Content rebuilt or added later takes the current state, filter and language. Example: wires rebuilt mid-run stay lit.
25. **Hovering never moves the page.** A hover preview does not change any size: reserve the room for its largest content. Example: the calendar's task list holds two lines, so its card stays 394 high whichever day is previewed. A data table keeps its height through paging, filtering and an empty result, and a gallery placeholder holds its image's real proportions before the image arrives. Moving between neighbouring items of one component switches directly, with no flash back to the original. Example: moving from the 14th to the 15th never shows the selected day's tasks in between. Story Forge Create alone follows decision [0010](../../docs/decisions/0010-story-forge-timeline-monitor-and-chat.md), decision 14: the owner ruled on 2026-10-31 that watching the monitor is a deliberate mode change. A click on the filmstrip, keyboard focus on the timeline itself, a playhead drag or playback enters that state, and the timeline's other tools do not, following decision [0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-timeline-click-to-watch.md); it may enlarge the monitor, widen the script column and temporarily push the side panel away, then restore the prior state. Only size properties transition, monotonically; toolbar and strip positions stay fixed. Chat focus alone does not move the browser or timeline; growing text and pinned history have the explicit layout effects in decisions 20 and 22. Where a monitor shows, watching also turns the lights off, in every scheme, following decision [0016](../../docs/decisions/0016-watching-turns-the-lights-off.md): the window goes black and only the monitor and timeline remain. Ordinary hover previews and all other samples keep this rule unchanged.
26. **Every canvas is infinite, and every side panel is an overlay.** A component's position belongs to the canvas: only the user's drag changes it, never a panel, a window resize or a fit. A canvas has no edge: drag empty space to pan, Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel to zoom about the pointer, and offer zoom and fit as controls; a plain wheel still scrolls a page around an embedded canvas. A panel is drawn above the canvas and takes no part in its layout. The view may give way, the world may not: when the content does not fit the uncovered area, zoom the view out, and return the view when the panel closes unless the user moved it. Example: opening Video's settings on a 1040px stage leaves Video at `x=590`; the view shrinks until it clears the 280px panel. Rule 9's Push never applies to a canvas. Bind through `UX.canvas` and `data-canvas`. See decision 0011.
27. **Dimming the surroundings is a switch; raising is not.** With the top bar's Dim others on, the default, the components around a focused one fade as these rules describe. With it off, they stay at full opacity and in the `rest` state, and the focused component only rises by the same shadow it always has. The switch covers fading that draws attention to another component: the dim state, unrelated blueprint steps, the crew around the one playing and the page behind open navigation. It does not cover fading that reports something else, such as columns that are not a drop target, filtered-out items and disabled controls. Example: with it off, selecting Camera on the node canvas leaves Mesh and Composer at opacity 1 and Camera on its raised shadow. Bind through `UX.dimOthers`. See decision 0013.
